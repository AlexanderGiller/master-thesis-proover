\section{Requirements}
\label{sec:requirements}

This chapter derives the requirements for the verifier from the ProoVer 2026 task and from the properties of TSTP derivations discussed in Chapter~\ref{sec:background}.
Section~\ref{subsec:req-context} fixes the task and its assumptions.
Section~\ref{subsec:req-scoring} analyses the scoring scheme, which determines how the verifier has to behave when it is unsure, and derives a decision policy from it.
Sections~\ref{subsec:req-functional} and~\ref{subsec:req-nonfunctional} list the functional and non-functional requirements.
Section~\ref{subsec:req-traceability} relates them to the modules and tests of the implementation.

\subsection{Task and Assumptions}
\label{subsec:req-context}

The verifier receives a FOF problem file and a TSTP proof file and must decide whether the proof is a valid refutation of the negated conjecture, that is, a derivation of \texttt{\$false} from the axioms and the negated conjecture \citep{proover-competition}.
The proofs use the roles \texttt{axiom}, \texttt{conjecture}, \texttt{negated\_conjecture}, and \texttt{plain} and the statuses \texttt{thm}, \texttt{esa}, and \texttt{cth}.
Some of the benchmark proofs contain deliberate errors, which are often small and easy to miss.
Only a small number of rules are specified by the organizers: Skolemization, the negated conjecture, and the import of axioms and conjectures \citep{proover-competition}.
All other steps may be checked by an external ATP, but only if they have status \texttt{thm} or \texttt{cth} and only with the step's premises.
The organizers state that handling edge cases is part of the task \citep{proover-competition}.

Let $P$ be a problem with axioms $A$ and conjecture $C$, and let a proof $\pi$ be a finite list of annotated formulas.
The verifier must decide whether $\pi$ is a \emph{valid refutation} of $P$:
(i) every \texttt{axiom} and \texttt{conjecture} step of $\pi$ is faithful to $P$,
(ii) the dependency graph of $\pi$ is acyclic and $C$ is used only by the step that negates it,
(iii) every other step is sound in the sense of Section~\ref{subsec:proof-objects-soundness}, and
(iv) $\pi$ derives \texttt{\$false}.
It answers \emph{verified}, \emph{failed}, or \emph{uncertain}, whose SZS statuses in the competition are \texttt{VerifiedGood}, \texttt{VerifiedBad}, and \texttt{Unknown} if the checker cannot decide, and \texttt{Timeout} if the time limit is exceeded \citep{ProoVer2026Proceedings}.
The competition only uses FOF input, so CNF and typed or higher-order input are out of scope.
We make the following assumptions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The input files are syntactically close to the TPTP BNF, but may be large (thousands of steps) and deeply nested.
  \item The problem file is trusted, the proof file is not.
  \item An external ATP may or may not be available at run time, and the verifier must behave safely in both cases.
  \item The organizers specify only a few rules and leave edge cases open. Where the specification says nothing, the verifier follows the conservative reading and never accepts without evidence.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scoring and Its Consequences}
\label{subsec:req-scoring}

In ProoVer 2026 a correct rejection of an invalid proof scores $+2$, a correct acceptance of a valid proof $+1$, giving up $0$, a wrong rejection $-1$, and a wrong acceptance $-10$ \citep{ProoVer2026Proceedings}.
The asymmetry has a direct consequence for the verifier's behavior in uncertain situations.
Let $p$ be the probability that a given proof is valid, given everything the verifier has found out.
The expected scores of the three possible answers are
\begin{align*}
  E[\text{accept}] &= p\cdot 1 + (1-p)\cdot(-10) = 11p - 10,\\
  E[\text{reject}] &= p\cdot(-1) + (1-p)\cdot 2 = 2 - 3p,\\
  E[\text{abstain}] &= 0.
\end{align*}
Accepting is therefore better than abstaining only if $p > 10/11 \approx 0.91$, and rejecting is better than abstaining only if $p < 2/3$.
For $2/3 < p < 10/11$, giving up is optimal.
So the verifier should not guess.
It accepts only if every step has been verified, rejects only if it has found a concrete defect, and it abstains in all other cases, for example if a required tool is missing, the prover gives no answer, or no checker exists for a rule.
A failed syntactic check on a rule with an ATP fallback is handled conservatively and reported as a defect unless the prover confirms the step (Section~\ref{subsec:design-atp}).
Figure~\ref{fig:expected-score} visualizes the three expected scores.
The probability $p$ cannot be observed, and the analysis does not try to compute it. Its purpose is to explain the asymmetric thresholds and the fail-closed policy, which leads to the soundness requirement below.

\subsection{Non-Functional Requirements}
\label{subsec:req-nonfunctional}

Table~\ref{tab:nfr} lists the non-functional requirements.
They do not describe what the verifier checks, but how it has to behave while checking. It has to stay safe when it is unsure, keep to the time limit, not crash on unusual input, and remain testable and extensible.
The most important one is NFR1, the fail-closed property, which follows directly from the scoring analysis of Section~\ref{subsec:req-scoring}. NFR2 and NFR3 follow from the competition setting with its time limit and its deliberately malformed proofs.
NFR6 and NFR7 concern the architecture and are evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:eval-modularity}.
\begin{table}[tbp]
\centering
\small
\caption{Non-functional requirements.}
\label{tab:nfr}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l P{0.82\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
ID & Requirement \\
\midrule
NFR1 & \textbf{Soundness (fail-closed).} A proof is verified only if every step has been validated. Missing tools, tool errors, unparsable output, and unknown rules never lead to acceptance. \\
NFR2 & \textbf{Time.} A proof must be processed within the 30-second wall-clock limit, including proofs with several thousand steps. The checker enforces its own limit and then reports \texttt{Timeout}. \\
NFR3 & \textbf{Robustness.} Malformed input, deeply nested formulas, and unexpected annotations must not crash the verifier or lead to an unjustified verdict. \\
NFR4 & \textbf{Determinism.} The verdict does not depend on process scheduling or on the order of independent checks. The only thing that varies between runs is the run time of the external prover. \\
NFR5 & \textbf{Diagnostics.} Every failure is traceable to a step and a reason (FR11). \\
NFR6 & \textbf{Extensibility.} A new rule can be added without modifying the other checkers. \\
NFR7 & \textbf{Testability.} Each checker is a pure function of the proof model and can be tested in isolation. The target for the line coverage of the checker modules is 85\,\%. \\
NFR8 & \textbf{Portability.} Python~3.11 or newer and the parser library \texttt{lark} are enough for the core. The ATP is optional and is configured through environment variables. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
