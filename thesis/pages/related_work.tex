\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

This chapter positions the thesis with respect to existing work on checking proofs of automated reasoners.
Section~\ref{subsec:rw-approaches} organizes the approaches by how a checker establishes the validity of an inference step.
Section~\ref{subsec:rw-proover} surveys the systems of ProoVer 2026, which address the same task as this thesis.
Section~\ref{subsec:rw-gdv-norgler} discusses the two most closely related systems, GDV and N\"orgler, in more detail.
Section~\ref{subsec:rw-other} briefly covers proof checking outside the TPTP ecosystem, and Section~\ref{subsec:rw-positioning} states how this thesis differs from the work discussed.

\subsection{Approaches to Proof Checking}
\label{subsec:rw-approaches}

Checkers for TSTP derivations can be grouped by how they decide that a single inference step is correct.

\paragraph{Semantic checking with external provers.}
The checker verifies the global and local structure of the derivation and then asks a trusted ATP to confirm that each step's conclusion follows from its premises.
Incorrect steps may additionally be refuted by a model finder, which searches for a countermodel.
This approach reuses mature provers and is applicable to any inference rule, but it increases the trusted computing base by the ATP and depends on its running time.

\paragraph{Internal replay.}
The checker contains its own implementation of the rules, for example of resolution or superposition, and replays each step without external tools.
This reduces external dependencies and can be fast, but every supported rule has to be implemented and trusted.

\paragraph{Translation to a logical framework or proof assistant.}
The derivation, or the output of an ATP checking its steps, is translated into a proof term or script that a small kernel of a proof assistant or logical framework verifies.
This yields strong guarantees, since only the kernel must be trusted, at the cost of translation effort and run time.

\paragraph{Hybrids.}
Practical systems often combine these strategies, for example by checking Skolemization steps structurally and delegating entailment steps to an ATP.
The systems in Section~\ref{subsec:rw-proover} illustrate the full spectrum.

Three observations are relevant for this thesis.
First, most entrants delegate entailment steps to external provers (E, Vampire) and use model finders such as Mace4 to reject wrong steps; only CheckProof and GAPT rely on internal reasoning engines.
Second, Skolemization is a recurring special case: several systems, including ProofGuard and PyCheck, check it structurally, with a freshness check on the introduced symbol, instead of via entailment, in line with the discussion in Section~\ref{subsec:normal-forms}.
Third, two systems, ProofGuard and PyCheck, are written in Python.
They are therefore the closest points of comparison for the implementation language and are discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:rw-positioning}.

\subsection{GDV and N\"orgler}
\label{subsec:rw-gdv-norgler}

\paragraph{GDV.}
The Derivation Verifier GDV is the established tool for checking TSTP derivations \citep{PLACEHOLDER_GDV}.
It verifies a derivation in four phases: structural checks, checks of the leaf formulas against the problem, rule-specific checks, and finally the verification of inference steps by calling trusted ATPs via SystemOnTPTP.
GDV is thus the reference for the semantic checking approach.
Its dependence on external provers makes it comparatively slow and ties its trust to the ATPs it calls.
GDV-LP~\citep{PLACEHOLDER_GDVLP} addresses the latter by translating proofs of individual steps into LambdaPi terms, so that the ATP no longer needs to be trusted.

\paragraph{N\"orgler.}
N\"orgler \citep{PLACEHOLDER_NORGLER} is a light-weight checker for TSTP refutations that follows the semantic approach of GDV.
It checks global and local structure, logical correctness, and faithfulness to the problem, uses an internal procedure for Skolemization steps, external provers for entailment, and a model finder to reject incorrect steps.
In the evaluation reported by its authors, it verified the same 1972 correct proofs as GDV, rejected more of 1952 falsified proofs than GDV in its best configurations, and was faster on average (0.084\,s versus 0.542\,s per proof in its fastest mode). % TODO: verify all numbers against the Norgler paper
These results show that a careful treatment of Skolemization and parallel checking can close much of the performance gap to internal replay, and they provide a useful baseline for the evaluation in Chapter~\ref{sec:evaluation}.

\subsection{Proof Checking Beyond TPTP}
\label{subsec:rw-other}

The idea of independently checking solver output is much older than ProoVer and is established in neighboring areas.
For propositional satisfiability, proof formats such as DRAT and LRAT allow solvers to produce certificates that small verified checkers validate \citep{PLACEHOLDER_DRAT}.
For SMT, the Alethe format and checkers such as Carcara play this role \citep{PLACEHOLDER_ALETHE}.
For first-order proofs, the Dedukti logical framework and its translators, for example from Zenon Modulo or Vampire output, enable verification by a small kernel \citep{PLACEHOLDER_DEDUKTI}.
In interactive theorem proving, proof reconstruction replays the output of external provers inside the kernel of a proof assistant, as in Sledgehammer for Isabelle/HOL \citep{PLACEHOLDER_SLEDGEHAMMER}.
GAPT \citep{PLACEHOLDER_GAPT} provides infrastructure for importing and transforming proofs of various provers.
These approaches generally give stronger guarantees than the direct checking considered here, but they require a proof format that is far more detailed than TSTP, or additional translation steps.
In contrast, TSTP derivations contain coarse inference steps whose justification is left to the checker, which is the setting of this thesis.

This thesis does not claim to be better than these systems in general.
Instead, it explores a particular trade-off: a small, modular Python checker in which every rule check is an independently testable function, internal checks are tried before any external prover is called, and the verdict policy is derived explicitly from the competition's scoring scheme (Section~\ref{subsec:req-scoring}).
Compared with GDV and N\"orgler, which send most entailment steps to external provers, it validates the structural, propositional, quantifier, and Skolemization steps itself and uses an ATP only as a fallback.
