\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

This chapter groups the approaches to checking proofs of automated provers, surveys the systems of ProoVer 2026 \citep{ProoVer2026Proceedings}, discusses GDV \citep{Sutcliffe2006GDV} and N\"orgler \citep{taprogge2026lightweight} in detail and positions this thesis.

\subsection{Approaches to Proof Checking}
\label{subsec:rw-approaches}

Checkers for TSTP derivations differ mainly in how they decide that a single step is correct.
\emph{Semantic checkers} verify the structure of the derivation and ask a trusted ATP whether each conclusion follows from its premises, as GDV \citep{Sutcliffe2006GDV} and N\"orgler \citep{taprogge2026lightweight} do. This works for any rule, but makes the ATP part of what has to be trusted.
\emph{Replay checkers} implement the rules themselves and recompute each conclusion, which is the standard for propositional proofs in DRAT and LRAT \citep{Wetzler2014DRATtrim,CruzFilipe2017LRAT}.
\emph{Certification} translates the derivation into a term for the small kernel of a logical framework such as Dedukti \citep{Assaf2023Dedukti,Delahaye2013ZenonModulo,Sutcliffe2025GDVLP}, which gives the strongest guarantee at the cost of translation effort.
Practical systems combine these strategies.
Outside TPTP, the Alethe format and the checker Carcara play this role for SMT \citep{Schurr2021Alethe,Carcara2023}, and Sledgehammer replays the output of external provers in the kernel of Isabelle \citep{Bohme2010Sledgehammer}.

\paragraph{Provers as step validators.}
\label{subsec:rw-vampire}

Vampire \citep{RiazanovVoronkov2002,KovacsVoronkov2013} plays two roles in this thesis.
As a \emph{step validator} it decides whether a single step follows from its premises, as in GDV and in most ProoVer entrants, and VaLeaDate additionally checks Vampire's output in Lean \citep{LeanOnVampire2026}.
A positive answer is evidence for a valid step, but a timeout says nothing. A verifier therefore cannot treat a missing answer as a rejection, and every call costs time from the limit of the proof.
As a \emph{proof producer} it is the subject of the transfer experiment in Section~\ref{subsec:disc-transfer}.

\subsection{Systems of ProoVer 2026}
\label{subsec:rw-proover}

ProoVer 2026 was held on 27 July 2026 in Lisbon, alongside CASC-J13 at FLoC~2026.
Ten systems took part, eight in the regular division and GDV and GDV-LP in a demonstration division (Table~\ref{tab:proover-systems}).
Most entrants delegate entailment steps to E or Vampire, and N\"orgler and ProofCheck also use the model finder Mace4 to reject wrong steps. Only CheckProof and GAPT rely on internal reasoning engines.
Several systems, among them ProofGuard and PyCheck, check Skolemization structurally instead of by entailment.
These two are also the only other entrants written in Python.

\paragraph{GDV and N\"orgler.}
\label{subsec:gdv}

GDV, developed by Geoff Sutcliffe, is the established verifier of the TPTP world \citep{Sutcliffe2006GDV,GDVSeminar}.
Its structural checks test that parents exist, that the derivation is acyclic and that refutations end in \texttt{\$false}.
For every formula it then creates a verification obligation that a trusted ATP discharges. A derived formula must follow from its parents, and a negated conjecture must be the negation of its parent.
For a Skolemized formula the obligation is reversed, and the parent is proved from the conclusion, which the documentation itself calls incomplete \citep{GDVSeminar}.
Without the Skolem annotations, GDV falls back to entailment in both directions, and the failure of this fallback does not reject the proof \citep{taprogge2026lightweight}.
To reduce the trust in the ATP, every system serves as the trusted system for the derivations of the others, and GDV-LP replaces the trust by LambdaPi certificates \citep{GDVSeminar,Sutcliffe2025GDVLP}.

N\"orgler \citep{taprogge2026lightweight} builds on GDV and organizes the checks into global structure, local structure, logical correctness and faithfulness (Table~\ref{tab:check-taxonomy}).
It checks Skolemization internally, uses E and Vampire for entailment and Mace4 to reject wrong steps, and adds checks that GDV lacked at the time.
Of 1976 correct and 1952 falsified FOF proofs from the TSTP library, GDV and all but one configuration of N\"orgler verify 1972 of the correct proofs, GDV rejects 1845 of the falsified proofs, and N\"orgler between 1845 and 1952, at 0.08 to 0.31\,s per correct proof against 0.54\,s for GDV \citep{taprogge2026lightweight}.
A direct comparison on the same benchmark was not possible, because these proofs use a different rule vocabulary than the ProoVer proofs.

Table~\ref{tab:check-taxonomy} compares the checks of GDV, N\"orgler and \TSTPy{}.
The checks NUF, DCP, APS and PIS make a proof \emph{informative} rather than correct, and N\"orgler omits them as well \citep{taprogge2026lightweight}.
The comparison was useful during development, because it showed that an earlier version of \TSTPy{} lacked the checks EIF and NAU.

\subsection{Positioning of This Thesis}
\label{subsec:rw-positioning}

This thesis explores one particular trade-off.
It is a modular Python checker in which every rule check is a small function that can be tested on its own, internal checks are tried before any prover is called and the verdict policy is derived explicitly from the scoring scheme.
Semantic checkers keep their own code small, but they rely on an ATP, a far larger program, even for simple steps such as instantiating a universal formula.
The internal checks of \TSTPy{} take the place of the ATP for such steps. The code that decides most steps is therefore the smaller one.
ProofGuard checks only the import steps, the negated conjecture and Skolemization internally and sends all other theorem steps to E, whereas \TSTPy{} checks \EvalRulesInternal{} rules internally and needs the prover for less than one percent of the derived steps of the benchmark (Chapter~\ref{sec:evaluation}).
