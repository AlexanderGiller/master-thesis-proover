\section*{Abstract}

{\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0.7\baselineskip}

Automated theorem provers are very effective, but whether their results can be trusted depends on an independent check of the proofs they produce.
This thesis presents the design and implementation of \TSTPy{}, a Python-based proof verifier for TPTP/TSTP proofs, developed for ProoVer 2026, a proof-checking competition held as part of the FLoC Olympics 2026 in Lisbon~\citep{proover-competition}.

The scoring scheme of the competition weights a wrong acceptance ten times as heavily as a correct one.
\TSTPy{} therefore accepts a proof only if every step has been validated, rejects it only if it has found a concrete defect and returns \emph{Unknown} otherwise.
Structural, propositional, quantifier and clausal rules as well as Skolemization are checked internally, and every failing step gets its own diagnostic.
The remaining steps are passed to the external prover Vampire, which only sees the premises of the step and never the proof as a whole.

Of the \EvalOffN{} problem/proof pairs of the competition, \TSTPy{} accepts all \EvalOffValid{} valid proofs, rejects \EvalOffRejInvalid{} of the \EvalOffInvalid{} invalid proofs and returns \emph{Unknown} for the remaining one, which gives \EvalOffScore{} of \EvalOffMaxScore{} points without a wrong acceptance.
The median run time is \EvalWallMedian{} per proof, well within the limit of 30 seconds.
A generated suite of rule-level test proofs and \EvalMutN{} mutants of valid proofs, of which every mutant that is no longer valid is rejected, suggests that the checks are not simply tuned to the benchmark, and even without the external prover no invalid proof is accepted.

\vspace{\baselineskip}
\textbf{Keywords}: Automated Theorem Proving, Proof Verification, TPTP, TSTP, Fail-Closed Checking, Skolemization, ProoVer 2026.
