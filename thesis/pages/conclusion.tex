\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis designed and implemented \TSTPy{}, a Python-based verifier for TSTP derivations for ProoVer 2026.
Starting from the asymmetric scoring scheme, it uses a fail-closed, three-valued verdict, checks most rules and Skolemization internally and delegates the remaining steps to Vampire under the restrictions of the competition.

\paragraph{RQ1.}
The ProoVer set uses \EvalRulesInSet{} rules, of which \EvalRulesInternal{} are checked internally, covering \EvalInternalPct\,\% of the derived steps and at least \EvalInternalMedianShare\,\% in half of the proofs.
The delegated rules are mainly the generic \texttt{consequence} and a long tail of rare rules.
Skolemization is checked internally, as the competition requires, and an entailment test could not replace this check, because a step with too few Skolem arguments still entails its parent. Its checks for scope, polarity and freshness are what rejects many of the invalid proofs.

\paragraph{RQ2.}
Every rule check is a small function of a step and its parents that can be tested alone, which the \EvalCheckerCov\,\% statement coverage of the checker package and the rule-level test suite reflect.
For most steps the trusted code is this checker package of \EvalCheckerLines{} lines rather than an external prover. A new rule still needs a branch in the driver, because the registry of the design is not implemented.

\paragraph{RQ3.}
On the \EvalOffN{} proofs of the benchmark \TSTPy{} reaches \EvalOffScore{} of \EvalOffMaxScore{} points without a wrong acceptance, with a median run time of \EvalWallMedian{} and at most \EvalRssMax\,MB of memory.
Every mutant that is no longer valid is rejected, and without the prover no invalid proof is accepted.
The run time is dominated by the start-up of the interpreter and, for very large proofs, by parsing. The exception is \texttt{PRV099}, whose pigeonhole step takes about 9\,s in the prover.

\paragraph{Future work.}
That \TSTPy{} does not yet handle unmodified Vampire proofs is to be expected, because a common vocabulary of inference rules is only now being established, and ProoVer is a step in this direction.
The limitations of Section~\ref{subsec:disc-limitations} suggest several directions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A registry-based dispatch in which a new rule needs only one entry and one function.
  \item Prover calls in parallel and with a short first limit. Cheap defects such as the one in \texttt{PRV099} would then be found before a hard step uses up the budget.
  \item A model finder that turns unresolved steps into defects, as in N\"orgler.
  \item A smaller trusted base, for example by requiring the external prover to produce certificates that a small kernel checks.
  \item Support for CNF proofs and the rule vocabularies of other provers.
\end{itemize}
